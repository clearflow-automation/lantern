02 — Credit where it's due

You're sitting on more than you think

The fix list below is long, and it would be easy to read it as "the brand is bad." It isn't. There are real assets here that most brands at this stage don't have.

  • BraFree™ is the best idea on your website. It names a real, unspoken problem, and nobody in Indian nightwear owns it yet. Right now it's buried in a product grid.
  • Sizes M to 4XL. Plus-size Indian loungewear is badly served. Brands that get fit right here earn unusually loyal customers.
  • Farshi co-ords are trending right now. Your catalogue is already deep in them. That's free demand waiting to be collected.
  • A mother-and-daughter story with real credibility. Health psychology from London, and twenty years of design. That story could carry your entire marketing — it currently sits at the bottom of the homepage behind a "Read More".
  • Two clean price tiers. ₹999 nightsuits and ₹2,600–₹3,900 co-ords. That's a working ladder; it just isn't being used as one yet.
  • GoKwik checkout. The right infrastructure choice for Indian D2C. Good call.

03 — What we found

Seven issues costing you orders

Ordered by how much each one is likely costing, not by how hard it is to fix. The first four are cheap.

01

"All sales are final" — on clothes with no size chart

Your return policy says you don't offer refunds, gives customers 24 hours to report a problem, and asks them to pay return shipping. Meanwhile you sell sizes M through 4XL with no size chart anywhere, and a review on your own homepage says the sizes run big.

See for yourselfReturn policy page: "we do not offer refunds. All sales are final." Exchange window: 24 hours from delivery Return shipping: paid by the customer Your own homepage review: "the size 3 XL is too big overall… I think ur sizes r big." Product page sizes: M / L / XL / 2XL / 3XL / 4XL — no size chart, no model measurements
What it costsFit is the single biggest reason people hesitate to buy clothes online, and you're answering that fear with "no refunds." Many shoppers read this page before checkout and close the tab. A 7-day exchange plus a proper size chart usually lifts conversion more than any advertising spend — and costs nothing but an afternoon of writing. It also protects you: "all sales are final" on consumer apparel is the kind of clause that invites complaints and payment disputes under India's e-commerce consumer rules.
02

The site takes 8.3 seconds to show anything

Your server is fast. The problem is what's being sent through it. One example: each "Shop by Category" tile loads an image 4,160 pixels wide to fill a space that is 249 pixels wide on a phone — roughly 280 times more image than needed, four times over. Add nine auto-playing videos and the homepage becomes 8 MB.

See for yourselfOpen punyaskriti.com on mobile data, not wifi. Count the seconds. Measured on a real cold load: First thing appears 8.3 seconds Page fully loaded 8.0 seconds Total downloaded 8.0 MB across 362 requests Auto-playing videos 9 on the homepage alone Category tile 4160 × 1517 image shown at 249 × 91 Mobile banner 374px wide, stretched across a 1125px phone screen (blurry)
What it costsRoughly half of mobile visitors abandon a page that takes more than three seconds. You are paying for traffic that never sees your products. This is about half a day of work and it's the cheapest improvement available to you.
03

Google doesn't know what you sell

The title of your homepage is just the two words "Punya's kriti." There's no description, so Google invents one. Your main headline is baked into the banner as a picture, which means search engines see an empty heading. And the part of your site code meant to list your social profiles is blank.

See for yourselfSearch Google for: cotton farshi co-ord set women Then search for: punyaskriti Notice you only appear when someone already knows your name. In your page code: Title "Punya's kriti" Description missing entirely Main heading empty Social profiles "" (blank) Blog 1 post, last updated 30 August 2025
What it costsEvery customer who searches "cotton nightwear for women" or "farshi co-ord set" and doesn't find you is a sale going to a competitor for free. Today you're only findable by people who already know your name — which means you pay for every single new customer, forever.
04

Emoji in product names are blocking Google Shopping

Several products have emoji in their names, and the emoji have leaked into the web addresses themselves. Google Shopping rejects product names with decorative characters — which means the highest-intent advertising channel available to you is closed until it's cleaned up. There are also live products named "untitled," and a spelling mistake running across every page.

See for yourselfProduct name: Pink Signature Badla Farshi Co-ord Set💫 Web address: /products/pista-pataka-farshi-co-ord-set%F0%9F%92%9A ← the 💚 is in the URL Live on your site right now, linked from the homepage: /products/untitled-aug9_01-11-33 /products/untitled-aug9_00-55-22 /products/untitled-jul29_20-11-00 Top bar of every page: "Buy Any 2 Sleewear At ₹1899/-" ← Sleewear
What it costsGoogle Shopping is where people search with their wallet already out. You can't run it until product names are clean. That's an afternoon of work standing between you and your best paid channel.
05

Your delivery promise contradicts itself

Product pages say "Same Day Or Next Day." Your shipping policy says 3–5 days to dispatch plus 5–7 to deliver — up to twelve days.

See for yourselfProduct page: "Same Day Or Next Day" Shipping policy: 3–5 business days to dispatch + 5–7 business days to deliver Free shipping: above ₹4,999 — nearly twice your typical order value
What it costsA customer who buys on the first promise and waits for the second becomes a complaint, a bad review, and often a refused delivery. One honest promise beats an ambitious one — "dispatched in 48 hours, delivered in 4–6 days" is credible and still competitive. And a free-shipping threshold nobody can reach isn't an incentive; at ₹1,499 it would actively pull a second item into the cart.
06

Cash on Delivery is invisible

You've installed GoKwik, which exists specifically to handle Cash on Delivery well. But nothing on your homepage or product pages tells customers COD is available. The ₹100 prepaid discount is advertised everywhere; the payment method most first-time buyers actually want isn't mentioned at all.

What it costsA large share of Indian apparel is bought on COD, especially at your price points and outside the metros. A first-time customer who can't tell whether she can pay on delivery often simply doesn't order. Adding "COD Available" next to your buy button is a ten-minute change — keep the ₹100 prepaid incentive, it's the right lever, just stop hiding the option that gets new buyers over the line.
07

The trust signals don't match the price

Your logo says "Your Everyday Luxury" and your co-ords are ₹2,900–₹3,900. Your contact address is a Gmail account. There's no Google Business listing, your Facebook page isn't linked from the site, and you're not on any marketplace. At this price, customers check.

See for yourselfContact email punyaskriti@gmail.com Facebook page exists, but not linked anywhere on your site Google Business not found Amazon / Myntra / Nykaa / Flipkart no listings found Social links in your site code blank
What it costsEach of these is a small doubt, and small doubts stack up into a closed tab. A branded email address comes free with your domain. Being on Myntra isn't about the margin — "also available on Myntra" is the fastest credibility you can buy.

04 — The bigger thing

There's a position here nobody has taken

One thing we noticed that isn't a bug. Your Instagram bio calls you a women's ethnic wear brand. Your website's own story calls you a nightwear and loungewear brand. Those are two different businesses, with two different customers and two different competitive sets — and neither one is quite what you actually make.

"Ethnic wear" puts you against Libas, Biba and a thousand other stores, competing on print and price. "Nightwear" puts you against the ₹499 shelf. What you actually make sits between them, and almost nobody in India merchandises it on purpose:

The gap

Pure-cotton Indian loungewear you can answer the door in.

Bra-free comfort. Sizes M to 4XL. Made by a mother and daughter. Not sleepwear, not going-out ethnic wear — the clothes an Indian woman actually spends most of her day in at home, and currently settles for.

Why it wins

It's a feeling, not a fabric

"Bra-free" and "presentable enough for the doorbell" are specific, emotional and shareable. "Pure cotton co-ord set" is a spec sheet. Emotional hooks are what make Reels travel and what make Instagram ads cheap.

Why it wins

It justifies ₹2,900

Nobody pays ₹2,900 for pyjamas. People do pay ₹2,900 for something they'll wear five days a week that looks good at the school gate and on a video call. Same garment, different occasion, different price ceiling.

Why it wins

The founders are the proof

A health-psychology graduate and a designer of twenty years, mother and daughter, building comfort wear. That isn't a bio — it's the reason to believe. Founder-led video is the cheapest high-performing creative in Indian D2C right now.

Why it wins

The category is growing

India's sleepwear and loungewear market was worth around USD 0.85 billion in 2025 and is forecast near USD 2 billion by 2034. It's growing faster than the brands inside it are professionalising — which is exactly the window a small, well-run house can walk through.

06 — A campaign that fits

"Ghar pe bhi acchi lagti ho"

One idea running across every channel for a quarter, instead of six unrelated offers competing for attention. The insight underneath it: Indian women dress well for everyone else, and wear whatever's left at home. The campaign gives that back — and it makes your tagline, Your Everyday Luxury, finally mean something specific.

Hero film · 30 seconds

The doorbell

A woman in an old faded nightsuit freezes when the bell rings. Cut. Same woman, same morning, in a Punya's Kriti co-ord — opens the door, takes the parcel, chats. No hiding, no rush.

Reels · weekly

Bra-free diaries

Short, funny, first-person. That moment you come home and unhook. This owns BraFree™ as a feeling rather than a feature, and it's the format most likely to travel for free.

Founder series

Maa–beti ki dukaan

Priyanka on why a block print takes three weeks. Punya on why comfort is a mental-health thing, not laziness. Shot on a phone, unpolished on purpose.

Always-on

Size 4XL, size M, same set

The same co-ord on five real women across the full size range, side by side. Kills the fit objection and does your plus-size targeting at the same time.

Festive

Maa ke liye

Ships as a gift box with a handwritten note, running through Navratri, Karwa Chauth and Diwali. Your highest-margin, most prepaid, least-returned basket.

Retention · WhatsApp

The second set

Day 21 after delivery: "How's the fit? Here's ₹300 off your second set." Nightwear is bought in pairs — most brands never ask for the second sale.

08 — Execution

What to do, in order

Week one — almost all of it free

  • Rewrite the return policy to a 7-day easy exchange, and publish a real size chart with garment measurements and model height.
  • Clean the product names — remove emoji, rename the "untitled" products, fix the "Sleewear" typo.
  • Write proper titles and descriptions for the homepage and all eleven collections, and put a real text headline on the banner.
  • Re-export images at the right size and cut the homepage videos from nine to three. Target under 2.5 seconds instead of 8.3.
  • Fix the delivery promise so the product page and the policy agree, and show "COD Available" near the buy button.
  • Install analytics and verify Google Search Console.

Weeks two to four

  • Move to a branded email address, link your Facebook page, create your Google Business listing.
  • Decide the position, and rewrite the homepage around it — banner, story, collection names.
  • Shoot the campaign: the doorbell film, three founder pieces, the multi-size set. Phone-shot is fine.
  • Submit your product feed to Google Shopping, now that the names are clean.
  • Set up the WhatsApp day-21 message and the abandoned-cart sequence.

Days 30 to 90

  • Turn on the advertising plan and hold it steady for eight weeks before judging it.
  • Seed 15–20 small creators, weighted toward plus-size and mother accounts.
  • Publish twelve blog posts against real search demand.
  • Launch the festive gift box — the next few months are the biggest gifting run of the Indian year.
  • List on Amazon and Myntra.
  • Get to 50+ reviews with photos by asking on WhatsApp after delivery. Fit reviews are what sell sized clothing.

After that

  • Open international shipping for customers abroad.
  • Launch the bridal and bridesmaid line into wedding season.
  • Consider a replenishment offer — nightwear is one of the few clothing categories where subscriptions genuinely work.
