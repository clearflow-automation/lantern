The site is better than most.
It still isn't selling.

nivedansharma.com is a competently built, genuinely good-looking site — cleaner than a lot of what agencies charge real money for. The problems aren't craft. 02 — Credit where it's due

What's genuinely good

Worth stating plainly, because the list after this one is long.

  • The homepage title tag is properly written. "Travel With Nivedan | Anagh Routes - Luxury India Tours" — 55 characters, brand plus category. Most small sites get this wrong; this one doesn't.
  • The mobile layout is clean. Proper responsive rendering, no horizontal scroll, readable type, a visible call-to-action above the fold. Checked on a real 320px viewport.
  • Speed is respectable. 2.5 seconds to load, 2.5 MB, on a platform that usually does worse.
  • Prices are published. Twelve tours with real USD prices and durations, $499 to $1,299. Most Indian operators hide this and lose the enquiry.
  • Destination pages have real depth. The Rajasthan page runs 1,200–1,400 words of genuine, specific detail — Mehrangarh, Dal Baati Churma, the 52 ghats at Pushkar. That's not filler.
  • Measurement is installed. Google Tag Manager, Meta pixel and visitor analytics are all live. He is at least able to see what's happening.
  • Schema, canonical and Open Graph tags are all present, and 13 of 15 homepage images have alt text.
  • The reviews, where they exist, are outstanding. The long German testimonial about travelling with an 85-year-old mother is one of the best pieces of sales copy on the site — and he didn't write it.

03 — What's costing bookings

Eight problems, ranked

01

Two different businesses are sharing one website

Seven of the twenty-three pages are a photography business — portraits, weddings, commercial shoots, portfolio, client albums, landscape. A German couple deciding whether to hand this stranger $3,000 and two weeks of their holiday lands on a site that also shoots weddings in Delhi.

The photography pages/portfolio · /portraits · /commercial-shoots · /weddings /travel-landscape-photography · /clients-albums · /services That's 30% of the site pointing at a different customer, a different price point, and a different purchase decision.
What it costsTwo things at once. It dilutes the trust signal at the exact moment a high-value traveller is deciding, and it splits Google's understanding of what the site is for. Photography should live on its own domain, or as one page linked from the About section — not as a co-equal half of the navigation.
02

The funnel breaks exactly where it should close

This is the single most expensive problem. The /india page lists twelve tours with prices and durations, but almost no detail. The /rajasthan page has 1,300 words of beautiful detail — and no price, no day-by-day itinerary, no dates, no inclusions, and no booking button. Neither page can actually close a sale.

See for yourself/india → 12 tours, $499–$1,299, durations. No itineraries. /rajasthan → 1,300 words of destination detail. No price. No itinerary. No dates. No inclusions/exclusions. No "book" button. A traveller who wants to buy has to email and wait.
What it costsEvery serious competitor publishes a day-by-day itinerary with what's included, what isn't, and a clear price. That page is what people forward to their partner and what Google ranks. Right now the two halves of that page exist separately and neither one converts.
03

Nothing has been written for search, ever

Twenty-three pages, zero articles. Inbound travel is won almost entirely on questions people ask before they're ready to book — and he has answered none of them. Meanwhile five of the twelve tours he actually sells don't even have a page of their own.

Sold on /india but with no page to rankMeghalaya · Holi Festival Tours · Yoga Tours Himalayan Trekking · Spiti Snow Leopard Expeditions "Spiti snow leopard expedition" is a low-competition search with genuine buyer intent. He sells it. He can't be found for it.
What it costsThis is the biggest growth lever on the list and the cheapest to pull. "Is India safe for solo female travellers", "best time to visit Rajasthan", "Golden Triangle 7 day itinerary" — these are searched constantly by exactly his customer. Twenty well-written articles would do more for bookings than any amount of ad spend, and unlike ads they keep working.
04

The About page's Google snippet is broken English

The meta description on /about — the sentence Google shows searchers — contains a grammatical error and an unverifiable superlative. On a page whose entire job is establishing that this person can be trusted with your holiday and your money.

Currently live/about meta description: "We Are the India's Most recommended Tailored Expedition Company. The leading innovator in private guided tours…" · "the India's Most recommended" — grammatical error · "Most recommended" — inconsistent capitalisation · The claim is unverifiable, and sits behind 8 Trustpilot reviews Page title: "About Anagh | Anagh Routes" · "Anagh" alone means nothing to someone who's never heard of him Body copy: ~1,900 characters — thin for the trust page of a $1,300 purchase
What it costsA European traveller comparing operators reads that snippet before clicking. Broad superlatives with a grammar mistake read as exactly the thing they're afraid of. Competitors say "Ministry of Tourism recognised, operating since 1999" — specific and checkable. That beats "most recommended" every time.
05

The domain and the brand are two different names

The website is nivedansharma.com. The brand — the logo, the schema, Instagram, Facebook, the Trustpilot profile name — is Anagh Routes. Every backlink, every review and every share is split between two identities, and the one in the domain is a personal name nobody searches for.

The splitDomain nivedansharma.com Logo & schema Anagh Routes Instagram @anaghroutes Facebook /anaghroutes Email anaghroutes@gmail.com Trustpilot listed as "nivedansharma.com"
What it costsNobody types "nivedan sharma" into Google unless they already met him. Either commit to Anagh Routes and move the site to anaghroutes.com with proper redirects, or fold the brand and go all-in on the personal name. The current halfway position means neither name is accumulating anything. Also: a luxury travel brand running on a Gmail address is a small, avoidable credibility leak.
06

Google is being shown almost no structure

The homepage has two H1 headings where there should be one, and only two H2s on the entire page. Section headers like "Why Anagh Routes" and "Enriching Lives Through Exceptional Travel Experiences" are styled text, not headings. And one money page has a title tag that's a sentence someone forgot to finish.

What it costsWith twelve priced tours he's eligible for rich results — price, duration and star ratings showing directly in Google. He's currently marked up as a generic local business, so he gets none of it. Fixing the titles and adding proper tour markup is a day of work with a permanent payoff.
07

The flagship testimonial is signed with a cartoon name

The homepage's hero review is genuinely excellent — long, specific, credible, and it names real places and real decisions. It is signed "Speedy Gonzalez, Germany." Separately, the site's Trustpilot link points to a personal user profile rather than the business review page, so the one click that would prove the reviews are real goes to the wrong place.

What it costsSomeone about to spend €2,500 with a stranger in another country reads that byline and pauses. Ask the reviewer for permission to use a real first name, last initial and city. And point the Trustpilot link at the actual review page — the reviews are real and good, they should be one click away.
08

He's invisible where foreign travellers actually look

No TripAdvisor listing and no Google Business Profile were found. For inbound India tours these are the two highest-trust surfaces that exist, and both are free. Eight Trustpilot reviews and 213 Instagram followers is not enough social proof to win a $1,300 decision against operators advertising 27 years and 14,000 guests.

What competitors lead with"Ministry of Tourism–recognised private tour operator" "Since 1999 · 14,000 guests from 40 countries" Exact-match domains: goldentriangletours.com, luxurygoldentriangle.com Anagh Routes states no credentials, registration or years-in-business anywhere on the site — despite having 12 years of experience.
What it costsHe has the experience and the happy customers; he just hasn't converted either into a visible signal. Every past guest is an unclaimed review. Getting from 8 reviews to 50, across Trustpilot, Google and TripAdvisor, would change the business more than any redesign.

04 — The SEO story

Findable by name, invisible to strangers

Short version: the technical foundation is solid, and there is nothing on top of it. Crawling, indexing, speed, canonicals and sitemaps are all fine — a search engine can read the site perfectly well. There just isn't much for it to read.

Searches for luxury India tours, private Golden Triangle tours and Rajasthan packages return operators with exact-match domains, two decades of history, and hundreds of pages of content. Anagh Routes appears for its own name and effectively nothing else. That's not a penalty — it's an absence.

The honest read

He can't win "India tour packages." He can absolutely win "Spiti snow leopard expedition."

Head terms are owned by operators with 27-year head starts and budgets to match. The winnable game is the long tail: specific trips, specific months, specific traveller types. That's also where the buyer intent is highest and the competition is thinnest — and it's exactly what a small, genuinely expert operator is built to serve.

07 — Campaigns

What to actually run

He is a solo operator competing with companies that have twenty-year head starts. Outspending them is not available. Out-specifying them is.

Free · highest impact

The review campaign

Every past guest, personally messaged, asked for a review on Google, TripAdvisor and Trustpilot. Going from 8 reviews to 50 changes the business more than a redesign would. Do this before anything else on the list.

Content

Twenty articles, one question each

"Is India safe for solo female travellers?" · "Best time to visit Rajasthan" · "Golden Triangle in 7 days" · "Travelling India with elderly parents." Answer them properly, from twelve years of actual experience. This is the compounding asset.

Product pages

One real page per tour

Day-by-day itinerary, price, what's included, what isn't, dates, photos, and an enquiry form on the page. Twelve of these, one per tour he already sells. This is the page Google ranks and the page people forward.

Video

Use the footage he already has

He's a working photographer with years of India footage and 213 Instagram followers. That's the most obviously wasted asset here. Short vertical video — one location, one story, thirty seconds — on Reels and YouTube Shorts.

Community

Be useful where people ask

Reddit's India and solo-travel communities are full of "can anyone recommend a guide" threads. Answer questions genuinely for months without selling. For a one-man operation this converts better than ads.

Paid · later

Long-tail search only

Don't bid on "India tour packages" — that auction is lost. Bid on "snow leopard expedition Spiti", "Holi festival tour small town", "India tour for elderly parents." Small budget, high intent, competitors mostly absent.

08 — Execution

Where the next 50 bookings come from

This week — free

  • Create the Google Business Profile and the TripAdvisor listing. Both free, both missing, both high-trust.
  • Message every past guest asking for a review. This is the highest-return hour of work available.
  • Fix the /about meta description — remove the grammar error and the unverifiable superlative, replace with something specific and checkable.
  • Fix the truncated /rajasthan title tag and write real titles and descriptions for all twelve destination pages.
  • Point the Trustpilot link at the business review page instead of a user profile.
  • Get permission to use a real name on the flagship testimonial.

This month

  • Decide the brand question — Anagh Routes or the personal name — and commit fully, with redirects.
  • Move photography off the main navigation onto its own domain or a single page.
  • Build a proper product page for each of the twelve tours: itinerary, price, inclusions, dates, enquiry form.
  • Switch the schema to TravelAgency and add TouristTrip and Offer markup per tour, plus AggregateRating.
  • State the credentials — years operating, registrations, guest numbers, countries served.

Next 90 days

  • Publish twenty articles, roughly two a week, each answering one real pre-booking question.
  • Start posting video weekly from the existing archive.
  • Build the German-market push: one partnership, translated key pages, German testimonials surfaced.
  • Turn on small-budget long-tail search ads once the tour pages exist to send them to.
  • Set up a simple post-trip email flow: review request, then a referral offer.
